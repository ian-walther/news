<h1>Editorial Synthesis, Evidence, and Corrections</h1>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Current product decisions. This is a living source note; revise it to current truth rather than appending contradictory updates.</p>

<h2>Purpose</h2>
<p>This note defines the Newspaper's neutrality goal, claim-level evidence model, interested-party handling, uncertainty language, citations, primary-source semantics, and correction/retraction behavior.</p>

<h2>Editorial Goal</h2>
<p>The product should deliver actual news separated, as far as a transparent evidence-aware system reasonably can, from partisan framing, editorial slant, clickbait, repetition, emotional manipulation, and source fragmentation.</p>

<p>This is not a promise of omniscient or perfectly objective truth. The defensible product promise is to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Separate reported facts from characterization, analysis, and opinion.</li>
  <li>Compare claims across sources.</li>
  <li>Prefer and expose primary evidence where available.</li>
  <li>Remove emotionally loaded and partisan framing from synthesized prose.</li>
  <li>Preserve real disagreement instead of averaging it into false balance.</li>
  <li>Preserve uncertainty rather than laundering repetition into confirmation.</li>
  <li>Keep material claims traceable to supporting sources.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Article Synthesis Rules</h2>
<p>The article should:</p>
<ul>
  <li>State well-corroborated facts directly and neutrally.</li>
  <li>Preserve material context needed to understand the development.</li>
  <li>Represent uncertainty and disagreement honestly in prose.</li>
  <li>Attribute unique, disputed, weakly supported, or interested-party claims.</li>
  <li>Remain a readable newspaper article rather than becoming an evidence table.</li>
</ul>

<h2>No Single Generic Trust Weight for Evidence</h2>
<p>A source's configured prominence weight cannot also stand in for claim-level truth. Evidence needs several dimensions:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Prominence weight:</strong> How strongly the configured source affects admission and story ranking.</li>
  <li><strong>Claim authority:</strong> What the source is actually positioned to establish.</li>
  <li><strong>Independence:</strong> Whether the source supplies independent evidence or repeats another source.</li>
  <li><strong>Interest or stance:</strong> Whether the source is an accused party, accuser, government agency, witness, analyst, advocate, or independent reporter.</li>
  <li><strong>Claim type:</strong> Observation, allegation, denial, official action, quotation, interpretation, prediction, measurement, or another explicit category.</li>
</ul>

<p>These dimensions prevent institutional status, publication volume, or a primary-source label from becoming a crude truth switch.</p>

<h2>Primary Sources Are Authoritative Only Within Scope</h2>
<p>A primary source can be high-confidence evidence of its own act or statement without being reliable evidence that all of its substantive assertions are true.</p>

<ul>
  <li>A court filing strongly establishes what was filed.</li>
  <li>An agency announcement strongly establishes what the agency announced.</li>
  <li>A company press release strongly establishes the company's stated position.</li>
  <li>An official's post strongly establishes that the official made the statement.</li>
  <li>None automatically proves every assertion contained within the document or statement.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Interested-Party Denial Example</h3>
<p>Suppose Company X is accused of wrongdoing, many outlets report evidence supporting the accusation, and Company X issues a denial. The denial is highly reliable evidence that Company X denied the allegation. It is not high-confidence evidence that the allegation is false because Company X is an interested party with a strong incentive to deny it.</p>

<p>A defensible synthesis may say:</p>
<blockquote>
  <p>Multiple outlets reported allegations that Company X did X, citing the available evidence. Company X denied the allegations in a statement Tuesday.[1] The statement did not independently resolve the disputed claims.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>The denial belongs in the article but must not mechanically balance or outweigh accumulated reporting. Conversely, repeated accusations must not be presented as proven merely because many outlets copied one unsupported claim. The article preserves the structure and limitations of the evidence.</p>

<h2>Coverage and Confidence</h2>
<p>Story prominence and claim confidence are separate:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Prominence:</strong> How broadly and strongly the event is covered within Ian's eligible configured source universe.</li>
  <li><strong>Confidence:</strong> How strongly a specific claim is supported and through how many genuinely independent evidence chains.</li>
</ul>

<p>Broad repetition can make uncertainty important enough to cover. It cannot turn uncertainty into confirmation.</p>

<p>The Newspaper should cover consequential uncertain reports as uncertainty rather than withholding them until certainty appears. Appropriate prose may state:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The claim originated with a named source.</li>
  <li>Other outlets repeated the report but did not independently confirm it.</li>
  <li>Officials have not commented.</li>
  <li>No primary evidence was available by the edition cutoff.</li>
  <li>Available accounts conflict.</li>
  <li>A statement comes from an interested party.</li>
  <li>Higher-weight sources had not covered or confirmed the claim by cutoff, without treating their silence as dispositive.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Citations and Claim-Level Provenance</h2>
<p>Detailed articles contain numbered citations or annotations for unique and consequential claims, followed by a citation list at the bottom.</p>

<p>Citations are especially appropriate for:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Claims unique to one source.</li>
  <li>Disputed or uncertain claims.</li>
  <li>Claims made by interested parties.</li>
  <li>Quantitative or unusually specific details.</li>
  <li>Direct quotations.</li>
  <li>Official statements and primary documents.</li>
  <li>Other details a reader may reasonably want to inspect.</li>
</ul>

<p>A numbered citation may identify multiple independently supporting sources. The citation list should preserve source name, article or document title, publication time, link, and a short evidentiary annotation when useful, such as “original report,” “official statement,” “interested-party denial,” or “not independently confirmed.”</p>

<p>The provenance surfaces remain distinct:</p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Coverage sources:</strong> who discussed the event.</li>
  <li><strong>Inline numbered citations:</strong> which claims require specific provenance.</li>
  <li><strong>Citation list:</strong> the exact material and evidentiary role.</li>
</ol>

<p>Common facts corroborated throughout the cluster need not receive a citation after every sentence. Readability remains a product requirement.</p>

<h2>Corrections, Clarifications, and Retractions</h2>
<p>Published editions remain artifacts and are never silently rewritten.</p>

<p>Future editions contain a separate <strong>Corrections, Clarifications &amp; Retractions</strong> section when necessary. Each notice:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Identifies and links to the original edition and article.</li>
  <li>States what was originally reported.</li>
  <li>States what is now known.</li>
  <li>Explains whether the change is a correction, clarification, or full retraction.</li>
</ul>

<p>The original article retains its original content. The hosted archive may display separate metadata or a notice around it indicating that a later correction exists and linking forward. This creates bidirectional linkage without modifying the historical text.</p>

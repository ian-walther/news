Product Decision Ledger and Open Questions

Status: Routing summary for settled product decisions and remaining specification work. Detailed rationale lives in the sibling product notes; this note should stay concise and current.

Settled Product Decisions

  1. The mature site has two first-class pieces: the article-centric Reading Feed and the event-centric Newspaper.
  2. Sources exist independently from outputs. Ingestion, zero-to-many generated RSS memberships, and Newspaper participation are separate controls.
  3. Newspaper policy inherits from logical outlet/intake-group defaults through input-feed, topic, and geographic overrides, with the most specific applicable rule winning.
  4. The canonical Newspaper is a hosted daily edition. PDF is deferred as optional vanity/export polish.
  5. Email is a lightweight delivery envelope containing the date, available sections, lead headlines, and one prominent edition link.
  6. The edition uses independently configurable content-cutoff and delivery times because processing may be lengthy.
  7. The delivery time is a hard deadline. The system automatically seals, publishes, and emails completed valid stories without manual review.
  8. Failures do not block successful stories; incomplete work is omitted visibly and late completions roll into the next immutable edition.
  9. Generation duration and phase-level processing metrics are first-class so cutoff and delivery times can be tuned.
  10. Edition length is determined by discovered events, not a fixed story or page count.
  11. The front page uses a neutral headline, compact factual paragraph, and coverage-source list.
  12. The full story is a clean synthesized newspaper article in prose, with evidence available at deeper layers.
  13. Personalization comes from explicit source, topic, weight, geography, and section configuration rather than reading-history tracking.
  14. Sources begin equally weighted and can be tuned globally, by feed, by topic, and for Local coverage over time.
  15. Source medium does not determine reliability; specialist accounts may outweigh institutional outlets for their beats.
  16. Single-source material normally stays in generated RSS Reading Feeds.
  17. Newspaper admission uses configurable weighted coverage with no mandatory high-trust anchor.
  18. A few low-weight sources may fail admission, while sufficiently widespread low-weight coverage can qualify.
  19. Coverage breadth and evidentiary independence are distinct: repetition can make a story important without confirming its claims.
  20. Exact duplicates and syndication collapse, while distinct outlets discussing one report may add prominence but not factual confirmation.
  21. Primary sources are desirable but not required. They are authoritative only within the scope of what they actually establish.
  22. Interested-party statements and denials are included and explicitly classified; they do not mechanically settle disputed substance.
  23. Evidence cannot use one generic trust number. Claim authority, independence, interest/stance, and claim type remain separate from prominence weight.
  24. Consequential uncertainty is covered as uncertainty rather than suppressed or presented as fact.
  25. Unique and consequential claims receive numbered citations with an annotated citation list at the bottom.
  26. Continuing stories contain discrete events and a claim/development timeline rather than one indefinitely growing cluster.
  27. Daily articles emphasize developments since the preceding edition, with optional Story So Far context.
  28. Reader timelines order primarily by event time while retaining publication and first-report time as provenance.
  29. The initial controlled sections are World, United States, Local, Business, Finance & Markets, Technology, Science, Health, Environment & Energy, Sports, Automotive, Arts & Culture, and Entertainment.
  30. Politics is a controlled tag rather than a section. Stories have one primary placement and secondary tags.
  31. The taxonomy uses evolvable stable reference records; later additions, renames, retirements, aliases, merges, and splits must not require broad rewrites or alter historical editions.
  32. Local is a geographic policy family based on an event occurring in or concretely affecting configured places, not on publisher location.
  33. Local-only Newspaper policy does not remove nonlocal articles from generated RSS feeds.
  34. A sufficiently high-weight Local source can qualify a geographically relevant singleton story, with explicit single-source provenance.
  35. Published editions are never silently rewritten. Later corrections, clarifications, and retractions link backward, while archived articles may show non-destructive forward notices.
  36. An edition publishes on time if at least one valid story completes. Zero completed stories create a failed run and failure notification rather than an empty or reused Newspaper.
  37. Edition generation is deterministic from a sealed manifest and has no mutable editorial draft. Inspection and retries are allowed; manual output edits are not.
  38. Weights are literal multipliers centered on 1.0. Overrides are absolute effective values, and the UI displays inheritance and effective weight.
  39. Normal nonlocal admission initially requires two distinct eligible Outlets and combined weight 2.0.
  40. One reporting Outlet plus a primary document can qualify when the document directly establishes the core event.
  41. Story lifecycle uses only Active and Dormant; related activity automatically reactivates the thread.
  42. Continuing stories show one sentence of visible context followed by a collapsed concise Story So Far with prior-edition and timeline links.
  43. The first Newspaper release uses configured RSS/Atom feeds and existing extraction tiers; Twitter, newsletter-specific ingestion, and automated official-source discovery are deferred.
  44. Outlet is a first-class real-world editorial identity, required parent of Input Feeds, outlet-wide dedupe boundary, source-vote unit, and policy scope.
  45. Input Feed is a discovery endpoint; Output Feed is a generated RSS grouping for consumption. Related reading content belongs in Output Feed membership, not a generic intake grouping.
  46. The existing Intake Group concept should semantically migrate to Outlet rather than survive as a separate abstraction.
  47. Article Source should become Article Appearance terminology so feed discovery remains distinct from evidentiary sources and reporting dependencies.
  48. Article classification is app-owned and article-level. Sub-feed membership and upstream categories are preserved as hints, not authoritative taxonomy.
  49. Extraction and classification move upstream of outputs so the enriched article pool exists independently of generated RSS membership.

Remaining Product Specification

  • Choose actual default cutoff, delivery time, timezone controls, and archive URL/navigation behavior.
  • Choose the permitted UI range and increments for source weights.
  • Define how partially supporting primary documents and reporting dependencies affect support metadata.
  • Define the Outlet/Input Feed migration details and app-owned classification artifact model in the repository implementation plan.
  • Choose the minimal Active-to-Dormant inactivity default and cluster-correction behavior.
  • Finalize claim types, source roles, interest relationships, and provenance vocabulary.
  • Define the deterministic correction workflow for errors discovered after publication.
  • Define the first implementation roadmap from Outlet migration and upstream extraction/classification through clustering, synthesis, hosted editions, and delivery.
  • Design the Outlet, feed, source-policy, locality, edition, story, citation, archive, and correction interfaces.

Implementation-Handoff Rule

Before Newspaper implementation begins, reconcile this Trilium product subtree into the repository's AI-agnostic forward-looking planning documents. The first implementation prerequisite is the opinionated Outlet migration plus upstream extraction and classification. Replace the current expansion boundary with an implementation-ready roadmap while preserving the existing generated RSS Reading Feed as a standalone product.
